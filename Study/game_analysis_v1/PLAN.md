# Coupled non-cooperative and cooperative analysis v1

19 September 2026. This stage uses the existing no-caching pilot. Both branches are required by the author's manuscript scope; this supersedes the earlier specification's “optional cooperative component” wording. No embedding, extraction or detector jobs, and no new transport sweep, are part of this analysis.

## Restricted strategic game

For each payload 0.1/0.2/0.4, environment E0/E1/E2 and attack budget 0.01/0.05, form an 8-by-5 payoff matrix from five-seed mean completion rates. Columns are the same five causal attack rules across every defender row. No attack is a separate control, not pooled across attack budgets. Analyse both transport completion and the recorded-extraction-weighted endpoint, separately.

Defender pure strategies are repetition policies meeting a hard extra-byte cap. Analyse 0%, 20%, 50%, 70%, 85%, 100%; 70%/85% are the explicit post-pilot trade-off refinements, not preregistered choices. The defender samples one feasible policy per session; the attacker chooses a rule before the session, knowing the mixture but not being handed its realised draw. The rule sees emitted traffic as in the pilot. The five-rule catalogue excludes adaptive inference/switching to another rule; any guarantee is relative only to that catalogue. It is not full-observation security against all causal attackers.

Solve max_x min_j sum_i x_i M_ij and the independent dual min_y max_i sum_j M_ij y_j with simplex constraints. Check nonnegativity, simplex sums, primal/dual values and restricted exploitability. This is a finite zero-sum matrix game, not a stochastic-game solution or an unrestricted Stackelberg result. Report solutions to numerical tolerance, not symbolic exact probabilities; optimal mixtures may be nonunique.

Compare with: no extra protection; best fixed maximin policy; uniform mixture over all feasible policies; equal expected per-channel extra-byte spending (maximise their common value under the same hard-feasible policy support); greedy positive marginal pure worst-case improvement per extra byte; and the cooperative Shapley-knapsack heuristic below. Include actual expected cost. Sharing a hard cap does not imply all comparators spend identical bytes. Equal-spending may collapse to the baseline where C3 repetition is infeasible, so the strong best-fixed comparator remains mandatory.

**Review addition before the full comparison run:** include a stronger balanced-randomisation comparator, maximising a common repetition probability across all three capabilities under hard-feasible policy support, without using payoffs. This differs from equal-byte spending because channel sizes differ. A preliminary independent solve suggested that it nearly matches the equilibrium; this addition is openly post-pilot and is not represented as preregistered.

## Cooperative value generated by the strategic game

Contributors are optional repetition capabilities R1/R2/R3, not the indispensable base channels or independent channel owners. Every coalition retains the three mandatory original objects and common authentication.

For S subset of {R1,R2,R3}, allow every budget-feasible pure policy whose extra repetitions are supported within S. Compute V_B(S) as that restricted minimax value, with the same attacker catalogue and budget. Define v_B(S)=V_B(S)-V_B(empty), so v_B(empty)=0. Coalition worth is a probability-point improvement, not money or available bytes. No resource penalty with unspecified units is added to the payoff; cost is constrained separately.

Compute all eight coalition values, Shapley contributions and subset interaction dividends. Verify efficiency, coalition monotonicity, the Shapley formula against enumeration of all six player orders, and known additive/symmetric/dummy toy games. Check core feasibility and whether the Shapley vector meets every coalition inequality as mathematical diagnostics only; do not assume transferable payments, superadditivity or coalition stability for this single-controller system.

For a concrete decision link, define a Shapley-knapsack heuristic: among feasible pure policies, maximise the sum of Shapley contributions of repeated capabilities; break ties by lower byte cost then lexical policy ID. Compare its actual worst-case payoff with the central minimax solution and best fixed policy. This heuristic does not assume that Shapley values themselves are budget allocations or that additive attribution reproduces coalition worth.

## Evidence boundary

All values are fitted to the development pilot; optimisation naturally favours its own objective and is not evidence of out-of-sample improvement. Preserve the five seed-level evaluations of each fitted policy against every fixed attack, but do not label them held out or use within-fit uncertainty as confirmatory inference. Freeze the fitted mixtures before subsequent fresh-seed transport evaluation. Do not draft final manuscript claims of improved reliability, security or Q1 novelty from fitted values alone.

Output model definitions, executable analysis/tests, fitted policy comparisons, eight-subset cooperative results, numerical checks and a concise findings report. Do not overwrite earlier plans or archived data.
